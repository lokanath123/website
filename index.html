I model how heat, charge and spin move through materials, starting from the atom.

I am a computational condensed matter physicist and Research Engineer II in the George W. Woodruff School of Mechanical Engineering at Georgia Tech, working with Prof. Satish Kumar on nanoscale thermal transport. My current work develops non-equilibrium and atomistic Green’s function (NEGF and AGF) methods for phonon and coupled electron–phonon transport, together with machine-learned interatomic potentials for interfaces and devices.

Before Georgia Tech, I was a postdoctoral researcher at UC Santa Barbara and Michigan Technological University, studying magnetocaloric and magnetoelectric materials, 2D materials and thermoelectrics with density functional theory, molecular dynamics and spin-lattice dynamics. This work has led to more than 40 peer-reviewed papers, in journals including Physical Review Letters, Physical Review B and Chemistry of Materials.

Current focus

Phonon quantum transport

NEGF and AGF simulations of interfacial and nanowire heat flow, including anharmonic scattering and coupled electron–phonon effects.

Machine-learned potentials

DFT-trained neuroevolution potentials (NEP) that bring first-principles accuracy to large-scale MD of interfaces and devices.

Spin-lattice dynamics

Coupled spin and lattice simulations of magnetocaloric materials, magnetostructural transitions and magnetic thermal switching.
